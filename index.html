The problem

Valid formats don't guarantee equivalent meaning.

Syntax and schema validation are essential. But converting analytical data across client, LIMS, and regulatory formats can introduce subtler changes that a checker examining either file on its own may not identify.

01 / RESULT SEMANTICS

Non-detect isn't one thing

“Not detected,” “less than,” and related flags may encode different statements. Simplifying them can lose a scientifically important distinction.

02 / IDENTIFIERS

Equivalent names, uncertain identities

Analyte names, CAS numbers, parameter codes, fraction and speciation fields don't always map one-to-one across formats.

03 / TRACEABILITY

Context travels with results

Detection limits, units, methods, qualifiers, and provenance need explicit treatment to avoid quietly dropping context.

First offering · In validation

Environmental Interoperability Audit

A focused, human-reviewed assessment concept for risks introduced as laboratory data moves between valid EDD and related reporting formats. Designed to complement—not replace—established validators and LIMS tools.

What we examine

  • Non-detect, less-than and qualification semantics
  • Detection, reporting and quantitation limits
  • Analyte and parameter identity mapping
  • Units, sample identifiers and provenance
  • Lossy transforms and human-review exceptions

What an early review looks like

  • Start with a short, no-data discovery discussion
  • Review public format specifications and synthetic examples
  • Identify a narrow, testable mapping question
  • Agree on terms and secure intake before any real files

No upload portal or direct access to customer systems is offered at this stage.

How we're approaching it

“Translate structure. Preserve meaning.”

Interoperability, not replacement.

LEXIXEL's public-specification and synthetic-record work examines cross-format semantic loss. It does not certify laboratory accuracy or regulatory compliance, and the approach has not yet been validated on customer-provided exports. The next step is feedback from professionals who handle multi-format laboratory data.
